Linear Actuator Guide

What is a linear actuator?

A linear actuator is a device that creates motion in a straight line. Instead of rotating like a motor shaft, it extends and retracts a rod to move a load forward or backward.

An electric linear actuator normally combines a DC motor, gearbox, screw mechanism, limit switches, housing, and extension rod. It is widely used when equipment needs controlled lifting, pushing, pulling, tilting, opening, closing, or positioning.

Simple electric control

Most 12V or 24V linear actuators can extend and retract by reversing polarity through a switch, relay, controller, or automation system.

Clean alternative to hydraulics

Electric actuators can reduce oil leakage, compressor noise, hoses, and maintenance in many light-duty and medium-duty motion systems.

Repeatable positioning

Optional Hall sensor or potentiometer feedback supports position monitoring, synchronized motion, and controlled automation.

Custom mechanical fit

Stroke length, installation length, mounting bracket, connector, cable length, speed, force, and protection rating can be adjusted for your project.

How to Choose

How to choose the right linear actuator?

Choosing the right electric linear actuator depends on the actual load, stroke, speed, duty cycle, installation space, working environment, control method, and expected lifetime.

Before requesting a quote, please confirm:

  • Required load force: N, kg, or lbs
  • Stroke length: how far the rod needs to extend
  • Voltage: 12V DC, 24V DC, 36V DC, or 48V DC
  • Speed requirement under load
  • Working environment: indoor, outdoor, wet, dusty, high temperature, or low temperature
  • Control requirement: simple switch, relay, controller, Hall sensor, or potentiometer feedback
  • Mounting space: retracted length, extended length, and bracket type

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about electric linear actuators

These answers help customers quickly understand actuator selection, applications, waterproof ratings, and customization.

What is the difference between a 12V and 24V linear actuator?

A 12V linear actuator is common for vehicles, battery systems, RVs, boats, and small equipment. A 24V linear actuator is often used in industrial automation, medical equipment, agricultural machinery, and systems that need more stable power distribution.

What does IP66 or IP68 mean for a linear actuator?

IP ratings describe protection against dust and water. IP66 is suitable for strong water jets and dusty environments. IP68 models are designed for higher waterproof protection, depending on the actuator structure and test condition.

Can a linear actuator stop at any position?

A basic actuator normally stops at the built-in limit switch positions. If you need intermediate position control, choose a model with Hall sensor, potentiometer, encoder feedback, or use an external controller.

What information is needed for a custom linear actuator quote?

Please provide load force, stroke length, voltage, speed, working environment, duty cycle, installation size, mounting method, feedback requirement, quantity, and any drawings or application photos.

Are electric linear actuators suitable for replacing hydraulic or pneumatic cylinders?

In many applications, yes. Electric linear actuators can reduce hydraulic oil leakage, air compressor requirements, hoses, and maintenance. However, very high force, very high speed, or continuous-duty applications should be checked carefully before replacement.
